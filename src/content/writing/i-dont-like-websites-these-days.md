I open a website because I want to read something.

Before I reach the first paragraph, I close a cookie banner, dismiss a newsletter modal, deny notifications, watch a video buffer in the corner, dodge a chatbot, and dig for the article under a sticky promo bar. By the time I'm reading, I'm already tired.

## What happened

Websites stopped being places to read and became machines for capturing attention. The shift was gradual enough that the people building them normalized it too.

The logic goes: a reader is a potential subscriber. A potential subscriber might click a button. If the button matters, make it bigger. If bigger is good, floating is better, and animated is better still.

The content ends up competing with the interface.

## The irony

These patterns exist to build an audience, to get people to return, subscribe, and share. But they're the same patterns that make reading unpleasant.

I don't share articles that interrupted me. I don't subscribe to popups. I don't return to sites that made me hunt for content.

I do return to sites where I could read without friction. Their restraint feels like respect.

## Why I love Hacker News

Most mornings, or late at night, I make coffee or tea and start exploring. Hacker News is simple, straight to the point. No popups, no modals, just a list of links and comments. Exactly what a doorway to good reading should be.

It's the same reason I use Linux. The terminal doesn't compete for your attention. It waits.

## Blogs I like

These are the kind of sites I keep coming back to, and the kind I want this site to be.

- ★ [Ibrahim Abou El-Anin](https://blog.ibrahimabouelanin.workers.dev/): mathematics, logic, and history of ideas, written in Arabic with care and illustrated with animated interactive visuals that make the thinking come alive.
- [Steph Ango](https://stephango.com/): minimal, writing-first, mixes software and personal topics.
- [Derek Sivers](https://sive.rs/): barely any design, mostly writing about programming, life, and books.
- [Julia Evans](https://jvns.ca/): a simple technical blog about things she's learning.
- [Mitchell Hashimoto](https://mitchellh.com/): quiet interface, the writing does the work.
- [Tania Rascia](https://www.taniarascia.com/): a developer site that doesn't feel like a portfolio.

What I take from them: the reader reaches the content immediately, with no ads, popups, animations, or engagement counters. Few colors, good typography, simple navigation. Personal, not corporate.
